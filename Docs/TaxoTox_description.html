TaxoTox

A Shiny Application for Taxonomic Mixture-Toxicity Assessment of Aquatic Pollutant Assemblages

Noam Gridish1,2, Yair Suari1

1The Hebrew University of Jerusalem 2School of Marine Sciences, Ruppin Academic Center, Mikhmoret, Israel

Abstract

TaxoTox is an open-source R/Shiny application for rapid assessment of the mixture toxicity of environmental pollutant assemblages. Given a table of measured compound concentrations (in ng/L) at one or more monitoring stations, TaxoTox (i) automatically resolves compound names to CAS Registry Numbers (CASRNs) using a curated internal lookup table augmented by optional PubChem REST API queries, (ii) retrieves taxon-specific median acute LC50/EC50 values from the US EPA ECOTOX Knowledgebase, (iii) computes per-compound Toxic Units (TUs) and summed Pollution Toxicity Indices (PTIs) separately for three functional taxonomic groups — algae, crustaceans, and fish — and (iv) presents results as interactive plots, searchable tables, and a downloadable multi-sheet Excel workbook. TaxoTox makes the TU framework accessible to environmental managers and researchers without requiring programming knowledge, and is designed for routine screening of monitoring data at the regional or national scale.

1. Introduction

Environmental monitoring programmes increasingly generate large matrices of compound-concentration data spanning tens to hundreds of organic micropollutants measured across many sampling sites and dates. Individually, most detected concentrations fall well below acute effect thresholds; however, the ecological risk of a complex mixture is not adequately captured by single-compound exceedance calculations. The Toxic Unit (TU) framework (Sprague & Ramsay, 1965; Könemann, 1981) addresses this by expressing each concentration as a fraction of its effect concentration and summing over all detected compounds to yield a single dimensionless mixture-toxicity index. When the sum of TUs — here termed the Pollution Toxicity Index (PTI) — equals or exceeds unity, the mixture may cause acute effects on the receptor taxonomic group; values between 0.1 and 1 are associated with potential chronic or sub-lethal effects on sensitive species.

Despite its conceptual simplicity, routine application of the TU framework is operationally demanding: compound CAS Registry Numbers must be resolved, toxicity benchmarks must be retrieved and harmonised across units, and results must be computed, visualised, and exported — repeatedly, for dozens of sites and sampling dates. TaxoTox automates this workflow within a graphical interface that requires no R programming knowledge, enabling its use by environmental managers and practitioners.

The application uses the US EPA ECOTOX Knowledgebase (US EPA, 2024) as its primary source of toxicity data — the most comprehensive freely available repository of aquatic ecotoxicology literature values — and is validated against independently computed PTI values from the US National Water Quality Network (Covert et al., 2020).

2. Scientific Background

2.1 Toxic Unit Framework

The Toxic Unit for compound i in sample j with respect to taxonomic group g is defined as:

TUijg = Cij / LC50ig

where Cij is the measured concentration of compound i in sample j (ng/L) and LC50ig is a reference effect concentration — the median lethal or effective concentration — for compound i and taxonomic group g (ng/L). TaxoTox uses the median of all LC50 and EC50 values available in ECOTOX for the given compound and group as the denominator. The median is preferred over the geometric mean because it is more robust to the long upper tail characteristic of ECOTOX distributions and because it does not require assumptions about the distribution of values.

Under the Concentration Addition (CA) model, which assumes that mixture components share a common mode of action or act independently on the same target, the mixture PTI for sample j and group g is:

PTIjg = ∑i TUijg

A PTI of 1 means that the mixture, collectively, is expected to be at the LC50 level for the group. The CA model is known to be a conservative (i.e. protective) predictor of mixture toxicity when components act via different mechanisms, and is recommended as a screening-level tool by US EPA and European regulatory guidance (SCHER et al., 2012; US EPA, 2000).

2.2 Risk Thresholds

PTI range Interpretation
≥ 1.0 Acute risk — the mixture may cause lethal or immobilisation effects on the receptor taxonomic group.
0.1 – 1.0 Chronic risk — sub-lethal or chronic effects on sensitive species are possible.
< 0.1 Low risk — the mixture is unlikely to cause measurable toxicity at the population level under the CA assumption.

These thresholds follow Sprague (1970) and are widely used in European water quality assessments (e.g. Stehle & Schulz, 2015; Moschet et al., 2014).

2.3 Taxonomic Groups

TaxoTox computes PTI separately for three functional groups that are standard ecological risk assessment endpoints:

  • Algae — primary producers; sensitive to herbicides.
  • Crustaceans — invertebrate consumers; sensitive to insecticides, especially pyrethroids and organophosphates.
  • Fish — vertebrate consumers; sensitive to a broad spectrum of organic pollutants.

Separating groups is important because the rank order of mixture risk frequently differs across taxa: a sample dominated by herbicides may pose negligible risk to fish but acute risk to algae.

3. Software Description

3.1 Architecture Overview

TaxoTox is implemented in R (R Core Team, 2024) as a single-file Shiny application (Code/app.R). The user interface follows Shiny's sidebarLayout convention: a left panel provides the three sequential action controls (upload, calculate, download), and a tabbed right panel displays instructions, CASRN matching, toxicity plots, and toxicity tables. All mutable session state is held in a single reactiveValues object, ensuring that the data flow through the application is explicit and testable.

The data pipeline has two phases: an offline preparation phase (run once by the administrator) that builds the toxicity reference database, and an online session phase (run every time a user uploads a file) that resolves CASRNs, looks up benchmarks, computes TUs/PTI, and produces outputs.

3.2 Offline Data Preparation

The toxicity reference data are pre-processed by taxotox_install.R and stored as a compressed FST file (Data/final_ecotox_data.fst) for fast loading at session start. The preparation pipeline is:

  1. Download ECOTOX. update_ecotox.R calls ECOTOXr::download_ecotox_data() to fetch the latest ECOTOX ASCII release from the EPA website and build a local SQLite cache (∼3–5 GB; updated approximately quarterly).
  2. Query and filter. A SQL query via RSQLite joins the results, tests, species, and chemicals tables, retaining only rows where the taxonomic group is fish, algae, or crustacean. Concentration is computed as the minimum across up to three reported concentration columns (conc1_mean, conc2_mean, conc3_mean), falling back to the midpoint of the reported range (conc1_min, conc1_max) when a mean is unavailable.
  3. Normalise units. ECOTOX uses heterogeneous unit strings including SI names (ng/L, µg/L, mg/L, g/L), common abbreviations (ppt, ppb, ppm, per-mille), and the "AI" (active ingredient) prefix. All are normalised to a canonical form and rows with unsupported units are dropped. Concentrations in units other than ng/L are multiplied by the appropriate factor (×103, ×106, or ×109) to yield conc_ng_L.
  4. Back-transform log-reported values. ECOTOX encodes some values as log10(concentration) under endpoint labels such as "(log)LC50". These are back-transformed (10^conc) before unit conversion.
  5. Filter to LC50/EC50. Only the two primary acute endpoints are retained. Decorators (asterisk, slash, log prefix) are stripped so that "LC50*", "(log)LC50", and "LC50" are treated equivalently.
  6. Write FST. The resulting flat table is written to Data/final_ecotox_data.fst at compression level 50. At session start, app.R reads this file and computes median(conc_ng_L) per (cas_number, ecotox_group) to form the denominator table.

As an alternative to ECOTOX medians, the companion script TaxoToxInstall_AFB.R builds an equivalent FST file using US EPA Aquatic Life Benchmarks (AFB/AIB/ANVPB) as denominators. This is useful for validation because EPA benchmarks were used by Covert et al. (2020) for the NWQN reference PTI calculation.

3.3 CASRN Resolution Pipeline

Matching compound names from user-supplied monitoring data to CAS Registry Numbers is a central challenge in automated toxicity assessment, because compound nomenclature is inconsistent across laboratories and reporting systems. TaxoTox resolves names in two sequential layers:

  1. Known_CAS (Layer 1 — instant, always on). An internally curated FST table (Data/Known_CAS.fst) of commonly monitored organic micropollutants, with standardised preferred names and validated CASRNs. Exact string matches (case-insensitive after trimming) are confirmed automatically with no user action.
  2. PubChem REST API (Layer 2 — optional). When the Web search checkbox is enabled, compound names not resolved in Layer 1 are submitted to the PubChem Compound REST API via the webchem R package. Candidate CASRNs are presented to the user in the CASRN Matching tab for review. No API key is required. An internet connection is required.

Compounds still unresolved after both layers are listed in the Manual CASRN Entry section. Users can enter any valid CASRN directly; confirmed entries are saved to a session file (temp_CAS.fst) for future integration into Known_CAS.fst.

All newly confirmed CASRNs — from Layer 1, Layer 2, or manual entry — are accumulated in the final_search_results reactive table that drives the toxicity calculation. The '2. Calculate Toxicity' button is programmatically enabled (via shinyjs) only once all uploaded compounds have been resolved or explicitly skipped.

3.4 Toxicity Calculation

When the user clicks '2. Calculate Toxicity', the server function:

  1. Joins the user's concentration matrix (wide format: compounds × stations) with the confirmed CASRN table on compound name.
  2. For each confirmed CASRN, retrieves the pre-computed median LC50/EC50 (in ng/L) from the denominator table, separately for each of the three taxonomic groups.
  3. Computes TUijg = Cij / median_LC50ig for every compound, sample, and group combination. Compounds absent from the denominator table (no ECOTOX data for that group) are assigned a TU of NA and excluded from the PTI sum.
  4. Sums TUs per sample and group to produce PTIjg. The result table includes both per-compound TUs and the total PTI column, formatted to three significant figures.

Calculations for all three taxonomic groups are performed in parallel (using purrr::map) and stored in a named list in the session's reactive state.

3.5 Outputs

TaxoTox provides three output channels:

  • Interactive plots (Toxicity Plots tab). Two panels of bar charts, rendered with ggplot2: (i) the top 10 riskiest samples ranked by descending PTI, one chart per taxonomic group; (ii) the top 10 most toxic compounds per group, ranked by their summed TU across all samples. Plots are generated reactively and update immediately after calculation.
  • Interactive tables (Toxicity Tables tab). Three DT DataTables (algae, crustacean, fish) with server-side pagination, column search, and export buttons. Concentrations and TUs are displayed as plain decimal numbers (3 significant figures). The PTI column is colour-coded by risk threshold: orange (PTI 0.1–1.0) and red (PTI ≥ 1.0) to support rapid visual scanning.
  • Excel workbook download. A multi-sheet .xlsx file produced by openxlsx:
    SheetContent
    Original data The uploaded concentration matrix (ng/L) as supplied.
    Algae toxicity Per-compound TUs and total PTI for each sample (algae benchmarks).
    Crustacean toxicity Per-compound TUs and total PTI for each sample (crustacean benchmarks).
    Fish toxicity Per-compound TUs and total PTI for each sample (fish benchmarks).
    CASRN Report All uploaded compound names with their resolved CASRNs, resolution source, and match status.
    Toxicity Coverage Binary matrix indicating, for each compound × taxonomic group combination, whether ECOTOX data were available to compute a TU.

4. Installation and Usage

4.1 Prerequisites

TaxoTox requires R (≥ 4.1) and the packages listed in Code/install_dependencies.R. All dependencies are available from CRAN. Install them by running: 

source("Code/install_dependencies.R")

The optional webchem package enables PubChem lookups; the app starts and runs fully without it (Layer 2 is silently skipped).

4.2 First-Time Setup

  1. Download ECOTOX data. Run Code/update_ecotox.R once. This calls ECOTOXr::download_ecotox_data(), downloads the current ECOTOX ASCII release from the EPA website (several hundred MB), and builds a local SQLite cache. Requires an internet connection and approximately 10–30 minutes.
  2. Build the toxicity reference file. Run Code/taxotox_install.R. This queries the local SQLite database, applies all filters and unit conversions described in Section 3.2, and writes Data/final_ecotox_data.fst. Requires approximately 5–15 minutes depending on database size.
  3. Launch the app. Open Code/app.R in RStudio and click Run App, or run: 
    shiny::runApp("Code/app.R")

Steps 1 and 2 need to be repeated only when a new ECOTOX release is available (approximately quarterly). Step 3 is run whenever the app is used.

4.3 Session Workflow

  1. Upload data. Click '1. Upload Samples Data' and select a CSV, TSV, TXT, XLS, or XLSX file. All concentrations must be in ng/L. Two layouts are supported:
    • Layout A (default): first column = compound names; subsequent columns = monitoring stations or sample identifiers.
    • Layout B: first column = station/sample identifiers; header row = compound names. The app transposes to Layout A automatically.
    After upload the app immediately resolves CASRNs and navigates to the CASRN Matching tab.
  2. Review CASRN matches. If the PubChem search was enabled, candidate matches appear in the table; review them and click 'Submit Selected Lines'. Any unresolved compounds appear in the Manual CASRN Entry section. Once all compounds are resolved, the '2. Calculate Toxicity' button becomes active.
  3. Calculate and export. Click '2. Calculate Toxicity' to run the PTI calculation. Explore results in the Toxicity Plots and Toxicity Tables tabs, then click '3. Download Results' to save the Excel workbook.

4.4 Input File Requirements

Important: All concentration values must be supplied in ng/L. The application does not perform input unit conversion; providing values in µg/L or mg/L will produce PTI values that are 103 or 106 times too small, respectively.

Missing values (NA, empty cells) are treated as zero concentrations and contribute nothing to the PTI. Non-detections should therefore be entered as 0 (or left blank) rather than at the detection limit, consistent with the approach of Covert et al. (2020).

5. Validation

TaxoTox has been validated against PTI values independently computed from the US National Water Quality Network (NWQN) pesticide concentration dataset (Covert et al., 2020). That study computed weekly PTIs at 38 NWQN stream stations (2013–2017) for fish (FISH-PTI) and cladocerans (CLADOCERAN-PTI) using EPA Aquatic Life Benchmarks as denominators.

For validation, the companion script TaxoToxInstall_AFB.R builds a reference dataset using the same EPA benchmarks (Freshwater Fish Acute, Freshwater Invertebrate Acute, and Freshwater Nonvascular Plant Acute) as denominators. The Python script Testing_Taxotox/TaxoTox_Validation.py automates the comparison: it pivots the NWQN concentration table to the wide format expected by TaxoTox, merges TaxoTox-computed PTI values with the published reference PTIs on a shared sample key (station ID + date), and produces regression scatter plots with OLS statistics for fish and crustacean groups.

Using ECOTOX medians (the default denominator) rather than EPA benchmarks produces PTI values on a different absolute scale, but preserves the relative ranking of samples and the identification of high-risk events. Users comparing TaxoTox output against published benchmark-based PTI values should use the AFB-based denominator file for a direct comparison.

8. Limitations and Future Work

The current version of TaxoTox has the following limitations that users should be aware of:

  • Input units. All concentrations must be provided in ng/L. Automatic unit detection is not implemented; supplying data in other units will produce incorrect PTI values without warning.
  • Denominator uncertainty. The ECOTOX database contains highly variable numbers of test results per compound and taxonomic group, from a single test to hundreds. The median denominator may be based on very few observations for less-studied compounds. The Toxicity Coverage sheet in the output workbook flags compounds where the denominator is absent (no ECOTOX data for that group) or based on a small number of tests.
  • Non-additivity. The Concentration Addition model assumes additive joint action. For mixtures containing antagonistic compounds the CA model will over-predict risk; for synergistic combinations it may under-predict. TaxoTox is intended as a conservative screening tool, not a substitute for compound-specific toxicity testing.
  • Marine toxicity data. The current ECOTOX filter retains all test results regardless of salinity regime. For estuarine or marine monitoring, a filter restricting results to saltwater test species may yield more ecologically relevant denominators.
  • CASRN resolution coverage. The Known_CAS table covers compounds commonly reported by European and US freshwater monitoring networks; compounds outside this set require PubChem lookup or manual entry.

Planned future developments include: automatic unit detection from file metadata, salinity-stratified benchmark selection for marine applications, time-series visualisation for repeated sampling at the same station, and batch processing of multiple input files.
